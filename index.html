Copy the routing and account numbers from a real check or your bank's website. Pick a starting check number well above your paper checkbook's numbers so the two never overlap.

Step 1: Measure your printer

iPhone printing adds its own margins and may shrink the page slightly. This step measures that so the check lands in the right spot. Use plain paper.

With a ruler, measure on the printed page (decimals or fractions like 1 1/16 both work):

Step 2: Check the edges

The bank code line must print 3/16 in from the bottom edge. Some printers cannot print that close to the edge. This sheet shows which edge lines your printer can reach.

If the 3/16 line is missing at the bottom but present at the top, turn on the option below and load check paper with the check end going in first (upside down).

Step 3: Fine tune

Print a VOID test on plain paper, hold it over a blank check sheet against a window, and nudge if needed.

Bank code font (MICR)

Symbol keys are the letters the font uses for transit, amount, on-us and dash, in that order. GnuMICR uses ABCD.

Backups

A backup is a single encrypted file. Save it to iCloud Drive or Files. It can only be opened with your passphrase.

Passphrase

Your accounts and check history are encrypted on this device with AES-256 using your passphrase. There is no recovery if you forget it, so keep it in your password manager.

Danger zone

Erases every account, the register, settings and the font from this device. Make a backup first.

Welcome

Create a passphrase. It encrypts your bank details and check history on this device. It cannot be recovered, so let iPhone save it to Passwords when asked.

Unlock

Forgot passphrase

Your data is encrypted with the passphrase, so it cannot be recovered or reset. You can either:

  1. Restore a backup, if you saved one, using that backup's passphrase.
  2. Erase this device and start over. This deletes the saved accounts and check register on this phone. Your printer settings and MICR font are kept.
